How PARALLAX tests a decision

PARALLAX compares two decisions after one controlled change to the tool evidence.

Same starting point. One change.

Capture. Record the tool response the agent received.

Fork. Keep the state this comparison can share.

Change. Modify one validated field.

Compare. Read the two decisions together.

Evaluate. Check them against the rule declared for the experiment.

The correct label is applied after the decision exists. It is never placed in the prompt. A hosted model can still vary after the fork.

PARALLAX harness

Captures the evidence, validates the edit, runs the comparison that execution supports, and writes the report. BYOA connects a compatible agent that already lives in a trusted local checkout. You define the policy. This website does not run an uploaded agent.

The provider-backed pilot and Decision X-Ray used the native research agent. The LangGraph and CrewAI examples are separate scripted local runs. Their labels are not the research oracle.

LangGraph here is a compiled graph with a checkpoint. It is not LangChain's higher-level agent API. A separate LangChain adapter is future work.

Three ways the comparison is made

These are three different measurements.

Native paired replay

The research engine copies one checkpoint into two arms. One arm keeps the captured observation. The other applies one validated edit.

LangGraph continuation

LangGraph resumes from a checkpoint. The graph pauses before the decision, and the adapter continues the original state and one edited fork.

CrewAI fresh rerun

CrewAI runs a fresh comparison. Each arm builds a new crew, and the two runs do not share a checkpoint.

PASS, FAIL, and INCONCLUSIVE

A local test uses your rules. A changed decision can still fail when you asked for a different label.

PASS

Both labels match the rules you declared, and the run checks passed.

FAIL

The run finished, and at least one label breaks a rule you declared.

INCONCLUSIVE

The pair cannot be scored. A timeout, a missing label, or a dropped edit is not a pass.

Policy used in the original studies

The research rule has three predicates. M is an error rate of at least 500 basis points. L is at least 10 errors, or severity error. D is a deploy inside the window. MONITOR means none of them. ESCALATE means all three. INVESTIGATE means any proper subset. A boundary value belongs to the breach.

Reference LangGraph and CrewAI examples use a different rule: ESCALATE at or above 500, INVESTIGATE at or above 200, otherwise MONITOR. Their labels are not the research oracle.

What a checkpoint does not answer

A checkpoint shows what happened after this observation changed. It does not show whether a fresh run would have called a different tool before the branch. CrewAI does not use that checkpoint mechanism at all.
